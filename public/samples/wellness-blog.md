# Slow Mornings

*A personal blog by Nandita Kulkarni — sleep, strength, appetite and the small habits that survive a deadline. Written from a third-floor flat in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. Since March 2023.*

---

## About the author

I spent nine years writing press releases for hospitals in Gurgaon, which taught me exactly how a health claim gets inflated. In 2022 my own labs came back with a fasting insulin number my doctor called "a warning you can still ignore", and I watched myself ignore it for eleven months.

So this blog is deliberately unglamorous. I am 34, I lift three times a week in a gym above a tyre shop, I sleep badly on long-haul work weeks, and I write about what actually stuck. No supplements to sell, no affiliate links, no before-and-after photographs. If you want evidence-heavy writing, read the papers I cite; if you want the honest middle — what a person does on a Wednesday when the study said something nicer — that's what's here.

I hold a master's in English from Pune University and a Level 1 strength coaching certificate from a Mumbai gym, obtained in February 2025 for reasons I explain in the second post below.

## Recent posts

### The 20-minute walk that beat my 6 a.m. run
**12 February 2026 · Sleep & routine · 7 min read**

For four years I told myself fitness meant running before sunrise, and for four years I did it about nine times a year. What finally moved my fasting glucose was embarrassingly small: a twenty-minute walk after dinner, phone left on the kitchen counter, usually along the Vellana channel road. My monitor showed a lower post-meal peak on walking nights, and I fell asleep forty minutes quicker. The exercise you repeat beats the exercise you moralise about.

### Two years of deadlifts, in an accountant's honest numbers
**24 November 2025 · Strength · 11 min read**

I started with an empty 20 kg bar and worse breathing. In the first week of November I pulled 92.5 kg for three, at 68 kg bodyweight, coached and filmed. This post is the ledger: the four exercises I never skipped, the two I dropped, the twelve sessions I missed in July when my mother was hospitalised, and the bloodwork that moved — triglycerides down from 212 to 138 mg/dL, HDL up nine points. Protein was never powder for me; it was two eggs, dal and a curd cup I actually weighed.

### Why I stopped counting calories and started counting protein
**6 August 2025 · Appetite · 6 min read**

Calorie counting worked for six weeks and then cost me a dinner with friends in Bengaluru that I spent mentally pricing. Switching to a protein floor — 1.4 g per kg, no upper limit on rice — gave me the same result with a fraction of the shame. South Indian meals are quietly low in protein unless you build them on purpose, so this is really a list: which tiffin items carry eggs, and how much dal is actually in a restaurant serving.

### My sleep tracker lied to me for eleven months
**19 April 2025 · Sleep & routine · 8 min read**

My band insisted I got 5 hours 40 minutes on a night I remembered sleeping through two alarm calls. It turns out consumer sleep staging is a guess dressed as a measurement, and the deep-sleep number it gave me made me anxious enough to buy a white noise machine I did not need. The real fixes were cheap: a cooler room, no screen in bed after 22:30, and stopping the morning review of the score. I sleep better without the app.

### Breathwork is not a personality, and other corrections
**3 February 2025 · Breath & attention · 5 min read**

I did 400 consecutive days of box breathing and my resting heart rate moved four beats per minute, which is well inside noise. Not a failure — the practice still holds my attention during the two hours before a school assembly — but I had absorbed the claim that breathing patterns rebuild a nervous system the way a mechanic rebuilds an engine. Here is what breathwork is actually good at, from a writer and not a clinician, plus the fortnight in 2024 when forced breathing gave me pins and needles.
