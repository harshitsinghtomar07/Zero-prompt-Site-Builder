------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY WE EXIST
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A child in a Marathi-medium government school in Igatpuri usually has fewer
than four books at home. Not books in English -- books, period. The school
library, where one exists, is a locked room with a register, a class monitor's
key and 600 titles, 40 percent of them outdated syllabus texts from 1998.

Vachan ("a sentence", and also "a promise") runs reading rooms inside
government schools as living spaces: open doors, floor cushions, Marathi
story books a nine-year-old can actually choose, and a trained local facilitator
present four days a week. We do not build parallel schools. We work inside the
school, with the headmaster, on the timetable, at the hours when the children
are already there. Our long argument with the district has always been simple:
children are not failing at reading because they lack intelligence; they lack
print, and they lack someone with time.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROGRAMS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. School Reading Rooms -- 27 rooms in 27 Zilla Parishad schools across 8
   tahasils. Refills of 120 new titles every two years, a facilitator for
   four days a week, and a lending register the children keep themselves.

2. Katha Hour -- a guided 45-minute storytelling block for Classes 1-3,
   run by two trained storytellers per school, three mornings a week.

3. Bhasha Bridge -- for children of migrant sugarcane and construction
   families who arrive speaking Hindi, Bhojpuri or Sindhi and must cope in
   Marathi. Ninety-minute after-school sessions, currently 212 children.

4. Chhaya Maths Circles -- peer tutoring for Classes 6-8, six students to one
   college volunteer, twice weekly. Started in 2024 in three schools, now 11.

5. Mobile Library Van -- one Mahindra Bolero, 2,400 books, seven haat
   (market) stops on a rotating weekly schedule in Dindori and Igatpuri.

6. Teacher Grants -- ₹40,000 per school per year, spent by the teachers
   themselves on books and shelf-making, with a one-page report. 19 schools
   received these in 2025-26.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
IMPACT, WITH THE YEARS ATTACHED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Marathi reading fluency in Grade 4 (our own assessment, same instrument
  since 2019): 41 correct words per minute at baseline in 2019, 63 wpm in
  2022, 78 wpm in the January 2026 round across 1,180 tested children.
* Books issued: 3,14,600 cumulative since 2017; 71,400 in 2025-26 alone.
* Children reached annually: 1,900 in 2019, 8,414 in 2025-26.
* Attendance where we run a room: 91.4 percent average in 2025-26 against a
  78.1 percent tahasil-level figure recorded by the block education officer.
* Girls in the room on any given day: 48 percent in 2026, up from 36 percent
  in 2020. Chhaya Maths Circles have the highest girls' share at 55 percent.

VOLUNTEER WITH US
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Storyteller -- two mornings a week, ₹300 travel reimbursement, 6-week
commitment minimum. Training day included.
Maths Circle tutor -- college students or graduates, two evenings a week,
11 weeks per term. Selected on patience, not on marks.
Library assistant -- 4 hours weekly in a school room; retires and homemakers
make the best ones.
Translator -- Hindi/Sindhi/Bhojpuri to Marathi for picture-book editions.
Remote, per title, ₹1,200 to ₹2,500.
Book repair clinic -- monthly Saturdays at the office, groups of six, we
bring the glue.
Data entry -- assessment sheets, from home, six hours a month.
Fundraising and events support for Katha Mela, July to October.
Currently 138 active volunteers; 62 give at least once a month. Write to
join@vachanreadingrooms.org with your tahasil and your availability.
